Universal travel adapter

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

An adapter lets a plug from one country fit a socket in another. It does not turn 220V into 120V. That difference matters when you leave Bangladesh for Dubai or the United States.

One adapter for both trips

The EPICKA universal travel adapter has US, EU, UK and AU plugs, one AC outlet, three USB-C ports and two USB-A ports. The seller says it does not fit Indian Type D sockets. It does not convert voltage.

EPICKA Universal Travel Adapter, 1 AC + 3 USB-C + 2 USB-A

Which plug you actually need

  • Bangladesh is about 220V. Sockets are often Type C, D, G or K. You do not need this adapter to charge at home.
  • Dubai and the UAE use Type G, the same shape as a UK plug, at about 220–240V. Use the UK plug on the adapter.
  • The United States uses Type A and Type B at about 120V. Use the US plug.

Look at the small print on the charger brick, not the cable. “Input: 100–240V” means the same brick can be used in Dubai and in the US once the plug fits. A brick marked only 220–240V needs a voltage converter in the US, which this adapter is not.

On the plane

A wall adapter does not help in the cabin if the seat has no socket you can use. A power bank with a built-in USB-C cable covers the flight. Power banks and spare lithium batteries go in the cabin bag only, never in checked luggage.
